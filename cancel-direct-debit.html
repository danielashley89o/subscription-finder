How to cancel a Direct Debit or stop a recurring card payment

Regular payments leave your account in three different ways, and each one is stopped differently. Here's how to tell which is which, and how to stop each one.

Which kind of payment is it?

  • Direct Debit: you've given a company permission to take money from your bank account. Common for bills, gyms, phone contracts and insurance. It usually shows as "DD" on your statement.
  • Standing order: you've told your bank to send a fixed amount on a schedule, often for rent or savings. It usually shows as "SO" or "STO". You control it completely.
  • Recurring card payment (a "continuous payment authority"): you've given a company your card details and let it charge you regularly. Most streaming services and apps work this way. These don't appear in your bank's list of Direct Debits.

Cancelling a Direct Debit or standing order

  1. Open your banking app or online banking.
  2. Look for a section called something like Payments, Direct Debits, Regular payments or Manage payments.
  3. Choose the payment and cancel it. If you can't find the option, phone your bank and ask them to cancel it.

Cancelling a Direct Debit stops future payments, but it doesn't end any contract you've signed. If you still owe money under a contract, such as a phone contract, the company can still ask you to pay another way. Tell the company you're leaving as well.

Stopping a recurring card payment

Cancel with the company first, through its website or app. If it keeps charging your card after that, contact your bank or card provider. In the UK you can ask your bank to stop recurring payments to a company on your card, and it should do so. Some banking apps let you block a company yourself.

Getting money back

  • Direct Debit Guarantee: if a Direct Debit is taken by mistake, for the wrong amount or on the wrong date, your bank should give you a full and immediate refund.
  • Card payments taken after you cancelled: ask your bank or card provider about disputing them.
