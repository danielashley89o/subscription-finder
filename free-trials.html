How to stop free trials turning into charges

Most free trials ask for your card details up front and start charging automatically when the trial ends. Companies count on people forgetting. These five habits make sure you only pay for what you actually want.

1. Put the end date in your calendar the moment you sign up

Set the reminder for two or three days before the trial ends, not on the day itself. That gives you time to decide, and to cancel before any charge goes through. Note the price it will switch to as well.

2. Check whether you can cancel straight away

Many services let you cancel during the trial and still use it until the trial ends. If yours does, cancel right after signing up and you can't be caught out. Check the service's terms first, because some end your access as soon as you cancel.

3. Sign up through your app store when you can

Trials started through the iPhone App Store or Google Play appear in one list, with their end dates and a cancel button. See how to find and cancel app subscriptions.

4. Search your email for "trial"

Trials don't show on your bank statement until they start charging, so your inbox is the best record of what you've signed up for. Search for trial, welcome and your plan.

5. Check your statement after the trial ends

If a charge appears that you thought you'd cancelled, contact the company straight away. If it won't refund you, your bank may be able to help: see how to stop a recurring card payment.
