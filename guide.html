How to find every subscription you're paying for

Streaming, apps, cloud storage, gyms, free trials that quietly started charging: subscriptions are easy to start and easy to forget. Here's how to find all of them, in about 15 minutes.

1. Go through 12 months of your bank statement

This is the only place that shows everything, because every subscription has to be paid from somewhere. Use a full 12 months: monthly charges show up quickly, but yearly renewals (Amazon Prime, antivirus, domain names, some apps) only appear once a year.

Look for:

  • the same company on roughly the same date each month;
  • small amounts that are easy to skim past, like £2.99 or £4.99;
  • vague names such as "APPLE.COM/BILL" or "GOOGLE", which can hide several different subscriptions;
  • prices that crept up during the year.

Doing it by hand takes a while. Subscription Finder does it for you: download your statement from your bank as a CSV file and drop it in. It runs in your browser, so your statement never leaves your device, and you can try it free on a sample statement first.

2. Check your phone's app store

App subscriptions are billed through Apple or Google, so they often show on your statement under Apple's or Google's name rather than the app's.

  • iPhone or iPad: open Settings, tap your name, then Subscriptions.
  • Android: open the Google Play Store app, tap your profile picture, then Payments & subscriptions, then Subscriptions.

Both lists show what's active, when it renews, and a button to cancel.

3. Check PayPal and Amazon

  • PayPal: some subscriptions are set up as automatic payments in PayPal, and your statement only shows "PAYPAL". Look in PayPal's settings for automatic payments to see every company you've let charge you.
  • Amazon: under Your Account, look at Memberships & subscriptions for Prime, Kindle Unlimited, Audible and channel add-ons.

4. Search your email

Search for words like receipt, renewal, subscription, trial and your plan. This catches free trials you've signed up for but haven't paid for yet, which won't be on your statement.

5. Cancel what you don't need

Cancel with the company first, through its website, app or app store (see how to cancel app subscriptions). Then keep an eye on your next statement to make sure the charges have stopped.

If a company keeps charging your card after you've cancelled, contact your bank or card provider. Card payments that repeat (a "continuous payment authority") can be stopped by your bank when you ask, and for Direct Debits the Direct Debit Guarantee lets you claim back payments taken in error. More in how to cancel a Direct Debit or recurring card payment.

6. Don't get caught by the next free trial

When you start a free trial, put a reminder in your calendar a couple of days before it ends. Subscription Finder can create these reminders for you, along with one before each yearly renewal.
